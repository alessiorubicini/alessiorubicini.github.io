# Alessio Rubicini — Personal Brand Guidelines

## Brand Identity

### Core Positioning

Alessio Rubicini is an **iOS developer who practices intentional restraint**. His brand communicates technical sophistication through what it removes rather than what it adds. Every element exists only if it serves clarity—there is no decorative filler, no gratuitous embellishment, no visual or verbal noise.

**Three Pillars:**

1. **Intentional Restraint** — Subtraction over addition. The courage to remove. What remains must earn its place.

2. **Editorial Precision** — Content is treated like a publication: strict hierarchy, measured pacing, and a rhythm that respects cognitive load. This is not marketing; it's a reading experience.

3. **Native-Craft Credibility** — Deep understanding of Apple platforms, from on-device AI to cryptographic security. The brand signals expertise through choices that only fellow developers recognize: SF Symbols conventions, iOS-native radii, platform-fluent typography.

### Brand Voice

**Primary Voice:** Calm, technically sophisticated, quietly confident

**Voice Attributes:**
- **Measured** — No hyperbole. Facts over fluff. "5+ years building" not "passionate expert."
- **Precise** — Specific technical terms used correctly (SwiftUI, Apple Intelligence, on-device AI).
- **Human** — First-person when appropriate. "I build" not "we deliver solutions."
- **Curious** — Questions invited. "Curious about why I build?" not "Learn my story."

### What the Brand Is NOT

- Not corporate or enterprise-speak
- Not hype-driven or buzzword-heavy
- Not visually cluttered or colorfully chaotic
- Not apologetic about technical depth
- Not afraid to show the work (code, process, failures)

## Visual Language

### Color Philosophy

**Warm Neutrals Over Stark Contrast**

The palette prioritizes reading endurance. No pure black or white—edges are softened.

| Role | Dark | Light |
|------|------|-------|
| Background | Ink (#111) | Warm Paper (#FBFBF9) |
| Primary | Warm White (#F0EEE9) | Near-Black (#1A1917) |
| Secondary | Muted Gray (#A8A49D) | Soft Gray (#4A4744) |
| Tertiary | Subtle (#706C67) | Faint (#8C8884) |

**Usage:**
- Primary for headlines and emphasis
- Secondary for body content
- Tertiary for meta, labels, timestamps
- Borders at 8-12% opacity—visible but weightless

### Typography System

**Dual-Typeface Strategy:**

- **Primary:** Geist (sans-serif) — Technical, modern, clean
- **Editorial:** IBM Plex Serif — Sophisticated, human, thoughtful

**Hierarchy:**

| Element | Typeface | Treatment |
|---------|----------|-----------|
| Headlines | IBM Plex Serif | 500 weight, tight letter-spacing (-0.01em to -0.015em), 1.1-1.15 line-height |
| Body | Geist | 400 weight, comfortable line-height (~1.6) |
| Labels/Meta | Geist | Uppercase, 0.04em letter-spacing, tertiary color |
| Code | Monospace | Slightly reduced size for alignment |

**Principle:** Tight, dense headings create visual gravity. Comfortable body text invites reading. The contrast between sans and serif signals technical competence + human thoughtfulness.

### Shape Language

**Softened, Not Rounded**

| Element | Radius | Rationale |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| Content | 8px | Professional softness |
| Icons | 10-12px | iOS-native recognition |
| Cards | 20px | Premium, elevated |
| Buttons | Fully rounded (pill) | iOS convention |
| Avatars | Circular | Human focus |

**Hairline Precision:** Borders are 1px (0.5px on retina). Visible but weightless. Physical precision matters.

### Spacing Philosophy

**Mathematical Rhythm**

All spacing derives from a single base unit (26px line-height) using multipliers:
- 0.25×, 0.5×, 0.65×, 0.75× for tight internal gaps
- 1×, 1.5× for component spacing
- 2×, 2.5×, 3×, 4× for section breaks

**Principle:** Visual silence through mathematical harmony. Every gap feels intentional.

## Content Principles

### Writing Style

**Structure:**
- Lead with the outcome, then the method
- Short paragraphs (2-4 sentences max)
- One idea per paragraph
- Progressive disclosure—don't front-load everything

**Language Patterns:**
- Active voice: "I built" not "was built"
- Specific over abstract: "Swift Student Challenge winner at WWDC25" not "award-winning developer"
- Technical precision: Use correct framework names (SwiftUI, not "Swift UI")
- Measured enthusiasm: "Thoughtful, human-centered apps" not "revolutionary world-changing products"

### Headline Patterns

**Editorial Headlines (Serif):**
- Tight letter-spacing (-0.01em to -0.015em)
- Medium weight (500)
- Compact line-height (1.1-1.15)
- Question or statement of purpose

Examples:
- "Hi, I'm Alessio Rubicini!"
- "Apps"
- "Curious about why I build?"

### Meta/Label Patterns

**Secondary Text (Small, Uppercase, Wide):**
- 13.5px size
- 0.04em letter-spacing
- Tertiary color
- Tabular numerals for dates

Examples:
- "5+ YEARS BUILDING"
- "WWDC25 WINNER"
- "BASED IN ITALY 🇮🇹🇪🇺"

## Interaction & Motion Philosophy

### Microinteractions

| Trigger | Response | Philosophy |
|---------|----------|------------|
| Hover | Subtle state change (underline, slight lift) | Affordance without distraction |
| Click | Clear completion feedback | Respect the action |
| Load | Staggered, choreographed reveal | Guides the eye naturally |

**Timing:**
- Entrance: Slow and deliberate (0.8s)
- Feedback: Immediate (0.2s)
- State change: Measured (0.25-0.5s)

**Easing:**
- Primary: `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1)` — Soft landing, Apple-style
- Playful: `cubic-bezier(0.175, 0.885, 0.32, 1.275)` — Elastic bounce (for personality moments)

### Reduced Motion Respect

All motion respects user preference. Animation enhances; it never blocks.

## Cross-Platform Application

### Social Media (Threads, LinkedIn, etc.)

**Visual:**
- Use warm neutral palette (no pure black backgrounds)
- App screenshots with 20px radius treatment
- Clean, uncluttered compositions
- Show the work: code snippets, App Store previews, SwiftUI previews

**Tone:**
- Share process, not just outcomes
- Technical breakdowns with specific code examples
- "Built this with X" not "Excited to announce..."
- Thread-style posts for longer technical explanations

**Format:**
- Lead with the visual (app screenshot, code)
- Short, punchy first line
- Progressive detail in replies/comments
- End with question or call to explore

### Presentations / Decks

**Layout:**
- Generous whitespace (2-3× content area)
- Left-aligned text (never center, except title slides)
- Grid-based alignment
- One concept per slide

**Typography:**
- IBM Plex Serif for headlines
- Geist for body and data
- Maximum 3 sizes per slide

**Color:**
- Dark mode default (#111 background)
- Accent sparingly (borders, not fills)
- Code blocks with subtle background (7% opacity)

### Video / Motion

**Pacing:**
- Deliberate, not rushed
- Hold on static frames longer than feels comfortable
- Transitions: Fade or subtle slide, never bounce or spin

**Typography on Screen:**
- Large, readable (minimum 48px equivalent)
- IBM Plex Serif for statements
- Geist for technical annotations

## Do's and Don'ts

### DO

✅ Lead with specific achievements ("Swift Student Challenge winner")  
✅ Use first person for personal voice  
✅ Show the code/process, not just the result  
✅ Ask questions to invite engagement  
✅ Use precise technical terminology  
✅ Maintain warm neutral palette  
✅ Apply mathematical spacing rhythm  
✅ Respect reduced motion preferences  
✅ End with clear next step or question  

### DON'T

❌ Use hyperbole ("revolutionary," "game-changing")  
❌ Center-align body text  
❌ Use pure black or white  
❌ Add decorative elements that don't serve content  
❌ Hide behind "we" when "I" is accurate  
❌ Use buzzwords without technical backing  
❌ Rush animations or transitions  
❌ Clutter compositions with unnecessary borders/shadows  
❌ Apologize for technical depth

## Brand in Practice: Example Applications

### Example 1: Social Post (New App Release)

**Visual:** App showcase card with 16:10 preview, 20px radius, semi-transparent background

**Copy:**
> Built a minimalist finance tracker for iOS. No subscriptions, no cloud sync—just you and your numbers.
>
> Tech stack: SwiftUI, SwiftData, local-first architecture.
>
> Link in bio. Curious what you'd track?

**Why it works:**
- Specific stack mentioned
- Anti-hype positioning ("just you and your numbers")
- Ends with question
- No exclamation points

### Example 2: Technical Thread (SwiftUI Tip)

**Visual:** Code screenshot with syntax highlighting, 8px radius

**Copy:**
> Three lines of SwiftUI that improve any list:
>
> 1. `.contentMargins(.horizontal, 16)` for safe area respect
> 2. `.scrollClipDisabled()` for edge-to-edge feel
> 3. `.scrollIndicators(.hidden)` for clean minimalism
>
> The details matter.

**Why it works:**
- Specific, actionable code
- Numbered, scannable format
- Ends with brand statement ("The details matter")
- No emoji spam

### Example 3: Bio / Tagline

> iOS Developer. Swift Student Challenge winner. Building thoughtful, native apps.

**Alternative (more personal):**
> I push Apple platforms to their limits, from on-device AI to cryptographic security. Always obsessed with native feel and detail.

**Why it works:**
- Specific credentials (WWDC25 winner)
- Technical depth shown, not claimed
- "Obsessed with detail" reinforces brand pillar
